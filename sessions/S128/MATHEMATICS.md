## 2. Adversarial non-pushout example — elementary mathematical proof (not a Lean homotopy formalisation)
Take N=2, symmetric graph with a single distinct edge {v₀,v₁}; let w(v₀)=(1,0), w(v₁)=(0,1), and choose any r with a<r≤b. At the Boolean grade p=(0,0), neither vertex is born; `K₀₀` contains only the empty face, so its barycentric realisation is the EMPTY topological space. At p=(1,0), only v₀ is born; its realisation is one point. At p=(0,1), only v₁ is born; its realisation is one point. At p=(1,1), both vertices and their edge are present; the realisation is the closed standard 1-simplex [0,1]. All maps are literal continuous inclusions and the square commutes.

The homotopy pushout of point ← empty → point is the disjoint union of two points (S⁰). The canonical map S⁰→[0,1] identifies the two endpoints as points of the interval, and induces a non-bijection on π₀: its source has two components, target one. It is therefore NOT a weak homotopy equivalence. Equivalently, quotienting the interval by its boundary endpoints produces a circle (relative cofiber of the union-to-terminal inclusion). Strictly commuting inclusions alone thus cannot identify the terminal stage with the homotopy pushout or recover a desired attaching cofiber. This is a genuine mathematical counterexample to the *shortcut*, not a counterexample to the universal D6 claim nor a Lean formalisation of π₀ or of the quotient circle.
