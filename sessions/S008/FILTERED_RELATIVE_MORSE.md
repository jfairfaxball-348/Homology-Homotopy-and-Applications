# S008-T24 — Relative filtered Morse naturality and its properness boundary

## Primary proof audit

Knudson–Scoville arXiv:2402.12116v2 (20 February 2026), §3 pp.6–7, Theorem 3.1, identifies the open-gradient Morse complex with the relative Morse quotient and Borel–Moore homology. Mischaikow–Nanda author-deposited original PDF, §§4–5 pp.7–9,12–13, already proves filtered chain equivalences over a PID for equal-birth acyclic unit-incidence matchings (Theorem 4.3/Proposition 4.4, Theorem 5.1). Not merely a field persistence statement. The proof constructs the chain maps by composing local cancellation maps and verifies commutation across stages. This closes the prior source-access gap, rather than creating novelty.

## Exact natural quotient statement (standard consequence)

Let (X_s,T_s) be finitely many nested compact finite cellular pairs, T_s⊂X_s, all subcomplexes of (X,T). Choose an acyclic unit-incidence matching on X that preserves EVERY X_s and EVERY T_s: each matched pair has equal membership in these subcomplexes. Algebraic Morse cancellation gives one chain contraction (i,p,H) on X restricting to each X_s and T_s. Indeed a local cancelled pair changes chains only through its unit boundary coefficient and faces, and membership/acyclicity ensure all correction chains stay in each subcomplex; compose these maps in an acyclic order. The equation ip−id=dH+Hd restricts, so it descends to the quotient C(X_s)/C(T_s). The inclusions commute with the same restricted i,p,H. Hence the relative Morse quotients form a naturally chain-equivalent diagram to C(X_s,T_s), over Z (or any PID with the stated units). This is precisely filtered algebraic Morse plus quotient naturality, not a new flagship.

## When those maps are actually Borel–Moore maps of open inclusions

Write K_s=X_s\T_s. Under the nested-pair assumption, K_s⊂K_t holds iff T_t∩X_s=T_s. If equality holds, K_s=X_s∩K_t is closed in K_t, hence a proper inclusion of locally compact spaces. The standard identification H^BM(K_s)=H(X_s,T_s) then is natural for these proper inclusions, using the maps of compact pairs. If equality fails, a point in (T_t∩X_s)\T_s lies in K_s but not K_t, so there is NO inclusion K_s→K_t whose Borel–Moore pushforward could be represented by the relative diagram. A general map of pairs still induces relative homology maps, but it cannot be called the inclusion-induced Borel–Moore map of those open complements.

Example with multiple deleted strata: take X_s always the same filled triangle. At the first index T_s is its boundary; at the next T_t is boundary plus a subdivided interior vertex. The corresponding open complements remove an additional point at the second stage, so the first open complement is not contained in the second. Both relative groups/chain maps exist, but there is no forward open inclusion. This is a geometric functoriality obstruction, distinct from S007's one-edge crossing cancellation. It is elementary and not claimed new.

Meaningful standard application: let T be a fixed closed union of frontier strata in a finite stratified complex, and let X_s be nested subcomplexes containing T. Then equality above holds at all stages, and a matching preserving X_s and T yields a Morse model for the proper Borel–Moore persistence of X_s\T. Equal entry levels for pairs inside the surviving strata and preservation of T suffice. Merely placing cells in the same stratum does not ensure equal entry levels or the filtered contraction. This application lies squarely in established filtered/relative Morse theory.
