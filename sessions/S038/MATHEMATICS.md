## T83 — real-axis rectangle projective dimension preservation (RE-AUDIT OF S018-T43)

Take finite chains G_i={s_i<c_{i,1}<...<c_{i,m_i}} included in R, with prebirth s_i STRICTLY below every generator and relation coordinate. Let G=G_1×G_2 and rho:R²→G send x_i to the greatest critical grade <=x_i, with all x_i<s_i clamped to s_i. Put E=rho^* and let V:G→Vect_F have ZERO stalk on every sentinel hyperface (as is the case for an input finite free presentation). Then E V=Lan_i V for the inclusion i:G↪R²; indeed both are V(floor(x)) on x>=s componentwise, and zero if any x_i<s_i. Both functors are pointwise exact on sequences of such modules.

For a one-dimensional (possibly open/closed, bounded/unbounded, nonprincipal) nonempty order-convex interval J⊂R, a grid point g∈G_i has initial downset D_g={x:rho(x)<=g}, equal to (-∞,c_next) or all R at the maximal grid grade. The left Kan stalk (Lan_rho F_J)(g)=colim_{x∈D_g}F_J(x). Since D_g is a directed ray with no last element, this colimit is F iff J∩D_g is cofinal in D_g (otherwise a later zero target annihilates every earlier generator), and is 0 otherwise. Write l=inf J, u=sup J in the extended real line. For a finite boundary c_next, this is exactly l<c_next<=u; for the final all-R ray it means u=+∞ and J is nonempty. Endpoint inclusion at l,u does NOT alter the formula, including a singleton at a cut: a singleton fails cofinality. The boundaries c_next strictly increase, so the nonzero grid stalks form one contiguous interval; the transition maps between nonzero stalks are the identity, proved by common representatives that survive to the larger ray. This proves Lan_rho F_J is a grid interval or zero.

For EVERY real rectangle Q=J_1×J_2, the indexing downsets are D_g1×D_g2 and its indicator is F_J1 external-tensor F_J2. Tensoring over a field preserves colimits separately. Thus
   Lan_rho F_Q = (Lan_rho1 F_J1) external-tensor (Lan_rho2 F_J2),
which is a finite G rectangle or zero. In particular, rectangles whose upper endpoints die inside a critical cell, rectangles with endpoint exactly at a critical boundary (open OR closed), lower cuts below s_i, finite upper bounds in the last unbounded cell, and genuinely unbounded axes are covered. A rectangle meeting the prebirth left tail may have a nonzero sentinel image, but the same Hom adjunction remains valid because E is rho^* and V is zero on sentinel hyperfaces. Do NOT replace Lan_rho Q by i^*Q: generally they differ.

The adjunction Hom_R²(Q,E W) ≅ Hom_G(Lan_rho Q,W), natural in W, shows that E carries ALL-relative-rectangle admissible short exact sequences of finite G diagrams to all-real-relative-rectangle admissible sequences. E sends every grid rectangle to a real rectangle (possibly with a left-unbounded sentinel interval); so E maps relative rectangle projectives to relative rectangle projectives. Conversely, restriction i^* sends any real rectangle to a grid rectangle/zero and is exact. For any grid rectangle R, its left Kan extension along inclusion i is also a REAL rectangle: each support coordinate is the half-open interval starting at its first grid point and ending at the NEXT grade past its upper grid point, or an unbounded tail if it ends at the last grade. By i_!⊣i^*, i^* therefore preserves ALL-relative-rectangle admissibility of arbitrary real sequences. In particular, applying i^* to any real relative projective resolution of E V proves the reverse pd inequality, while applying E to any grid relative resolution proves the forward inequality. We obtain, for every k>=0,
    pd_{rect(R²)}(rho^*V)<=k  iff pd_{rect(G)}(V)<=k.
The converse remains valid if an ambient relative projective is a direct summand of a finite sum of rectangles: restriction preserves direct summands; no Krull–Schmidt inference is necessary. For possible unbounded resolutions, the equality is interpreted as equality of minimal finite lengths or both infinite. **This is the earlier T43 theorem in n=2, independently audited here; no new priority.**

## T84 — binary-bit k=1 algorithm on genuinely real R² rectangles

Input B bits: a homogeneous free presentation of a nonnegatively Z²-graded F2[x,y] module, with q generators and r homogeneous relation columns, all graded exponents encoded in BINARY, and coefficients of homogeneous monomials in F2. The associated real multipersistence module is the quotient of free principal real upsets at the generator degrees by the homogeneous relation maps. Allow signed integer/rational binary grades if total-order comparison and a strictly smaller binary-encoded sentinel are available. Choose critical chains consisting of each distinct generator/relation coordinate plus a prebirth sentinel. They have <=q+r+1 grades on each axis, **independent of the magnitude of exponent integers**. All stalk quotient spaces and transition maps are computable by F2 elimination: homogeneous monomial shifts do not require exponent-by-exponent expansion. The sentinel is zero on EVERY hyperface.

The all-real-rectangle pd test is EXACTLY finite-grid all-rectangle pd by T83 (historical T43). There are at most [a(a+1)/2][b(b+1)/2]=O((q+r+1)^4) grid rectangles. For each rectangle R with lower corner s, Hom_G(R,V) is the kernel in V(s) of the at-most-two exit maps just past R's upper sides (or no exits when unbounded in a finite coordinate). Construct the finite universal rectangle evaluation cover
    P0 = direct_sum_R R ⊗ Hom_G(R,V) → V.
It is a pointwise epi (principal upset summands), and Hom(R,-)-epi for EVERY rectangle by construction. Its pointwise kernel K is finite, explicitly computed and part of an admissible exact sequence. Relative Schanuel in the exact category gives pd_rect(V)<=1 iff K is rectangle-projective. The latter is equivalent to a finite-grid rectangle decomposition, recognized by the original Botnan–Lebovici–Oudot 2020 algebraic weak-exactness equalities for all s<=t:
    im K(s→t) = im K((t1,s2)→t) ∩ im K((s1,t2)→t)
    ker K(s→t) = ker K(s→(t1,s2)) + ker K(s→(s1,t2)).
All tests are Gaussian elimination over F2. There are polynomially many Hom systems, rectangle summands, square tests and intermediate matrix entries, with every F2 entry one bit. Thus the resulting **decision procedure is polynomial in input BIT length B** for this fixed n=2, k=1, F2 binary-grade, homogeneous finite-free-presentation subproblem with the collection of **ALL real axis-aligned rectangles**, including open, closed, below-first-grade, nonprincipal and unbounded cuts. It is NOT a complexity claim for arbitrary n,k, arbitrary input formats, arbitrary coefficient fields, arbitrary interval collections, or all simplicial multifiltrations. This is a proof of algorithm existence, NOT implemented end-to-end or externally refereed.
