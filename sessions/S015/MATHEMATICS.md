## A. T37 — fixed-dimensional finite-grid block-relative projective dimension one

Fix a computable field F and **fixed** n≥2. Let G=∏_{i=1}^n [m_i] with each m_i≥2 and s=|G|; input consists of explicit F-vector spaces M(p), bases and matrices for all generating arrows, satisfying square commutativity, with total dimension d=Σ_p dim M(p). Let I_G be **exactly** the finite set of all LLO n-blocks (birth, death, recursively induced full-axis blocks; Definition 3, for n=2 the two bands with one full axis and arbitrary interval on the other). Put B=|I_G|, X=add{F_I:I∈I_G}.

Use the **relative exact** structure E_X: 0→A→E→C→0 is pointwise exact AND Hom(F_I,E)→Hom(F_I,C) is surjective for *every* I∈I_G. Define pd_X(M) as the shortest length of a resolution by objects in X with all short pieces E_X-admissible.

**Theorem T37.** There is a deterministic finite-field-arithmetic algorithm deciding pd_X(M)≤1 in O_n(s^9(d+1)^4) field operations (generous upper bound; NOT optimised) and polynomial memory. If YES it returns an explicit E_X-admissible length-one resolution and a finite Koszul vanishing certificate for its first syzygy. If NO it returns an explicit Koszul middle-homology obstruction in a k-cube (2≤k≤n) to projectivity of that syzygy. The same procedure decides pd_X(M)≤k for each fixed k by iterating canonical covers k times (polynomial with exponent depending on k). The statement concerns **expanded finite-grid field diagrams**, NOT unrestricted/succinct R^n presentations or arbitrary infinite interval families.

**1. Actual block enumeration and enough projectives.** Every LLO block is a product of (a) upper rays on ≥2 active axes and whole inactive axes, (b) lower rays similarly, or (c) an interval on a single axis and whole other axes; duplications removed. This description is equivalent to the finite-chain recursive definition. For fixed n, enumeration uses B≤C_n s², since each upper/lower active-axis choice has ≤s choices and each one-axis interval ≤m_i(m_i+1)/2≤s². Each principal upset G_{≥p} belongs to I_G: use a birth block on active axes and full-axis inductions; if ≤1 active axis, a band (including the full interval). Thus every finite G-module has a pointwise surjection from a sum of blocks, and X is the actual additively generated relative projective class.

**2. Explicit canonical cover.** For each I solve the linear naturality equations for Hom_G(F_I,M). There is one vector variable f_p∈M(p) for p∈I, arrows inside I impose f_q=M(p≤q)f_p, and arrows from I to its complement impose M(p≤q)f_p=0. Outside→inside imposes no extra relation because the source stalk is zero. All comparable arrows may be imposed, or use generating arrows with commutativity; the system is linear with ≤d unknowns and polynomially many equations. Choose a basis f_{I,1},...,f_{I,h_I}. Form
P(M)=⊕_{I∈I_G} F_I^{h_I},   ε_M:P(M)→M
whose components are the chosen natural transformations. This is a pointwise epimorphism: every representable principal upset F_{G≥p} is in X and the evaluation Hom(F_{G≥p},M)≅M(p) supplies every vector of M(p) at p. Moreover every Hom(F_J,M) map lifts to the J-summands of P(M) by expansion in the chosen basis. Therefore ε_M is an **E_X-admissible epimorphism**, not merely a surjection in the ordinary diagram category; P(M)∈X.

Compute K(M)=ker ε_M as a full commutative diagram by pointwise nullspaces and restriction maps (naturality makes K stable under arrows). There is an E_X-exact sequence
0→K(M)→P(M)→M→0.

**3. Relative Schanuel, exact iff.** If pd_X(M)≤1, choose any E_X-exact 0→Q_1→Q_0→M→0 with Q_i∈X. Pull back P(M)→M and Q_0→M; their fibre product has split E_X-exact projections because the respective kernels Q_1 and K(M) and respective target projectives give liftings. The resulting relative Schanuel isomorphism is
K(M) ⊕ Q_0 ≅ Q_1 ⊕ P(M).
Since X=add(blocks) is closed under retracts in the finite-dimensional Krull–Schmidt category, K(M)∈X. Conversely if K(M)∈X, its exhibited admissible exact sequence is an X-resolution of length ≤1. This is the **only-if and if** and handles pd=0 and M=0.

**4. Koszul rank test for actual relative projectivity.** On every axis-aligned k-cube C of G, 2≤k≤n, form the LLO Koszul cubical chain complex K_C(K(M)), with chain groups ⊕_{|S|=i}K(M)(vertex indexed by S), ordered orientations and alternating signed edge maps; maximal vertex is degree 0. Determine the ranks of all differentials. Return YES precisely when
dim H_i(K_C(K(M)))=0  for every C, every 2≤k≤n and 0<i<k.
This is equivalent to K(M) being block-decomposable **by original LLO arXiv2402.16624v4 Definition 9 + Theorem 14**, hence to K(M)∈X; no ordinary free-pd substitution. A NO certificate consists of C,i and an explicit cycle outside the boundary image, verified by two rank computations.

**5. Polynomial size and complexity.** Each Hom(F_I,M) has dim≤d; total copies h=Σ_I h_I≤Bd and each stalk of P(M), hence K(M), has dimension ≤Bd. There are O_n(s²) axis-aligned subcubes (including all lower-dimensional face choices), each with at most 2^n vertices and bounded-length Koszul complexes. Gaussian/rational linear algebra on at most O_n(Bd) dimensions per complex costs O_n((Bd)^3) field operations; the total is O_n(s²(Bd)^3)≤O_n(s^8(d+1)^3). Solving B naturality systems, assembling P and computing K is bounded by the advertised larger O_n(s^9(d+1)^4). Field element operations are unit-cost; over Q use polynomial-bit exact matrix algorithms with input bit lengths accounted for, not this unit-cost bound. For fixed n, the number of steps and all intermediate matrix sizes are polynomial in the expanded grid input; fixed k iteration is also polynomial (degree grows rapidly). Do NOT claim a uniform polynomial bound when n or k is part of the input.

**6. Genuine one-critical simplicial multifiltration corollary.** Suppose a finite simplicial complex with N simplices has an n-component birth vector g(σ) for each simplex, g(face)≤g(σ), and the grade-u complex is exactly {σ:g(σ)≤u}; n fixed. Compress to the ≤N+1 critical levels per coordinate, giving s≤(N+1)^n. For each grid grade, compute the boundary complex over F, choose kernel/image bases in homological degree j, and compute natural maps induced by actual inclusion of chains. This yields a full finite-grid diagram M=H_j(X(-);F) in polynomial arithmetic time and with d≤sN. Apply T37. Thus for genuine **one-critical** simplicial bifiltrations n=2, and similarly fixed n, the **finite critical-grid-relative** pd_X≤1 question is polynomial-time. This does NOT identify the critical-grid exact structure with an unrestricted real-parameter exact structure or cover all compressed presentation models.

**7. Hostile explicit square tests.** On G=B2 with vertices b=00,a=10,c=01,t=11:
(a) The simple S_b has value F at b and zero elsewhere. It IS a death block and pd_X(S_b)=0. The usual representable-principal-upset resolution has pd_ordinary(S_b)=2, so the wrong exact structure would give a false negative.
(b) Define L(b)=0 and L(a)=L(c)=L(t)=F with both maps a→t,c→t equal to 1 (other maps zero). The LLO 2-middle complex is 0→F²→F→0, differential (x,y)↦x−y; H_1=F, so L is not block-projective (pd_X(L)>0). Yet
0→F_{ {t} }→F_{ {a,t} }⊕F_{ {c,t} }→L→0
is exact with kernel top inclusion z↦(z,−z), and is Hom_X-exact: each 2D block belongs to the finite list of birth/death/full-axis band supports; a map from one of these blocks to L either vanishes (a block including b forces all subsequent components to vanish), or factors through the appropriate top/right/left band summand; the top singleton maps lift as well. Hence pd_X(L)=1. In particular a non-block module need not have relative pd>1.

## C. T38 — single finite metric and graded Rips realisation of T36; exact limitation

**Proposition (internal, classical universal-metric construction).** S014's particular strict induced flag B3 diagram can be made one-critical on the VERTICES: a finite flag complex K is equipped with one grade g(v)∈B3 per vertex so that X_u=K[{v:g(v)≤u}]. Indeed in the underlying compatible CW diagram, original bottom cells first appear at b, the three disk cells at e_i, the three pair-ball cells at e_i+e_j, and terminal cylinder/target cells at t; every cell has a UNIQUE minimal occurrence. Compatible relative triangulation can respect these nested subcomplexes without crossing their mutual intersections, so each simplicial face has a unique earliest grade. After barycentric subdivision, the new vertex [σ] has grade equal to the earliest grade of its face σ; a chain σ0⊂⋯⊂σr appears exactly when σr appears. Hence the induced-subcomplex property and one-critical vertex grades hold.

Now assign one metric on V(K):
d(v,w)=0 if v=w, =1 if {v,w} is an edge of K, =2 otherwise.
Triangle inequality holds because the largest nonzero distance is 2 and the sum of any two nonzero distances is ≥2. Since K is FLAG, for EVERY u∈B3 the Vietoris–Rips complex VR_{3/2}({v:g(v)≤u},d) is EXACTLY X_u, not just homotopy equivalent. All T36 integral homology/homotopy persistence, its kη comparison and cofiber cup square persist in these metric-subset Rips diagrams. If d is perturbed by sup norm <1/2 on pairwise distances and the radius remains 3/2, edge/nonedge status remains unchanged. Encode grade coordinate g_i(v) as height 2g_i(v), with thresholds 1 (for u_i=0) and 3 (for u_i=1); perturb each height by <1 and selected vertices are unchanged.

This gives a **fixed arbitrary finite intrinsic metric**, fixed Rips scale and THREE independent superlevel/sublevel vertex thresholds, with an exact quantitative edge/threshold margin. It is NOT a theorem for a Euclidean metric of bounded dimension, NOT a genuine two-parameter (Rips-scale, one function) bifiltration, NOT a bound on vertex count as a function of |k|, and NOT a new HHA-sized Hopf theorem: the 1/2/flag metric realisation is generic for every finite flag complex. If a compatible triangulation before barycentric subdivision has T nonempty simplices, the constructed Rips point cloud has exactly T vertices; no independent k-uniform bound on T is claimed.

**Order-theoretic warning:** B3 cannot order-embed into a product of two chains. Put a_i={i}, b_i=[3]\{i}; then a_i≰b_i but a_i≤b_j for i≠j. Any order embedding into product of two chains requires some coordinate c_i with a_i(c_i)>b_i(c_i). Two of the three c_i coincide; with i≠j in the same coordinate, a_i≤b_j and a_j≤b_i imply a_i(c_i)≤b_j(c_i)<a_j(c_i)≤b_i(c_i)<a_i(c_i), impossible. Thus a naïve reindexing of the whole B3 cube as an order-reflecting subset of a two-coordinate parameter grid cannot yield the required two-parameter result. This is an indexing obstruction, NOT an impossibility theorem about genuinely new two-parameter topological examples.
