# C07 — proof-level 2026 Borel–Moore comparison and exact filtration obstruction

Knudson–Scoville, arXiv:2402.12116v2 (Feb 2026), final Topology and its Applications 388 (Sept 2026), 109867, §3 Theorem 3.1 at preprint PDF page 7: for finite K=X\T, Morse homology H_i(M(K))≅H_i(X,T)≅H_i^BM(K). Proof uses short exact 0→M(T)→M(X)→M(X,T)→0 with open critical cells identifying the quotient; examples include single open n-cell. This is ALREADY PUBLISHED, not a C07 invention; available arXiv full proof inspected, final publisher article abstract and metadata inspected, pagination/wording equivalence not certified.

S007-T19 (PROGRAMME_PROVED elementary obstruction): X a closed interval with endpoints v0,v1 and oriented edge e, T={v0}; K=X\T has relative cellular C1=Z e → C0=Z v1 by d(e)=v1. Filter by F0=( {v0,v1},{v0} ), containing v1 but not e, F1=(X,T). The acyclic Morse matching (v1,e) cancels all the relative cells of K, so the UNFILTERED relative chain complex is contractible. But H0(F0)=Z, while the zero Morse complex has H0=0 at F0. Therefore no filtration-preserving chain homotopy equivalence to the globally zero Morse complex exists for these filtration indices. Both global and stage-wise chain groups/free boundaries are explicit, with no enumeration.

A standard sufficient repair requires matched cells to enter at equal filtration grade (with usual acyclicity/unimodular incidence), allowing a filtered algebraic Morse contraction. This is substantively covered by Mischaikow–Nanda, 'Morse Theory for Filtrations and Efficient Computation of Persistent Homology', DCG 50 (2013) 330–353; theorem-level original abstract available, detailed proof not rechecked.
