## T217: an actual family of finite cyclic regular-cover ghosts, with a parity criterion

Let p be prime, q=p^r>=2, Q=C_q=<g>, k=F_p, and B=k[Q]=k[t]/(t^q), t=g-1. Define N=sum_{j=0}^{q-1}g^j=t^(q-1) in B, by (1+t)^q=1+t^q in characteristic p. Thus tN=Nt=0, ann(t)=NB and ann(N)=tB. In the integral ring Z[Q] retain g-1 and N_Z=sum g^j: (g-1)N_Z=N_Z(g-1)=0. **Do not** replace integral alternating boundaries by t,N without first reducing to k.

For m>=0 and a tuple of units (w_0,...,w_m) modulo q, act freely on S^(2m+1)⊂C^(m+1) by g(z_0,...,z_m)=(ζ^{w_0}z_0,...,ζ^{w_m}z_m). All nonidentity powers act without fixed points because every weight is a unit mod q. The quotient is a weighted lens space L^(2m+1)(q;w). Pull back its standard one-cell-per-dimension lens CW structure to the genuine regular cover S^(2m+1)→L, with exactly one *free Q-orbit* of cells in each degree 0,..,2m+1. A is the full free orbit of 0-cells. Integral cellular chain boundaries are d_(2j)=N_Z and d_(2j+1)=g^(u_j)-1 for j>=1, with each u_j a unit modulo q (choice/convention of lens weight and cell lifts may change u_j or unit signs). Their composites vanish *integrally* since (g^u-1)N_Z=0. After tensoring k, g^u-1=t v_u(t), where v_u(0)=u mod p is invertible. An invertible B-basis change brings the relative chain complex to the length n=2m+1 perfect complex P_n: B in degrees 1,...,n, d_i=N for even i and d_i=t for odd i>=3. The basis changes preserve scalar N because B is commutative.

Collapse A in the free pair to a Q-fixed basepoint W=S^(2m+1)/A and form the reduced suspension Z=ΣW. The positive-dimensional suspension cell orbits have trivial stabilisers; the single basepoint has full stabiliser Q. This is a finite BASED Q-CW complex, NOT a globally free Q-space after collapse; the original pair (S^(2m+1),A) consists of free Q-spaces. A based fixed point is needed for the specific equivariant pinch/fold construction; no universal impossibility of alternate free-pair models is claimed. The genuinely continuous Q-equivariant map
  f_N=fold ∘ (∨_{j=0}^{q-1} g^j) ∘ pinch_q : Z→Z
is defined by iterated reduced-suspension co-H pinch, all deck maps (Q abelian), and fold. On suspended reduced k-cellular chains its class is multiplication by N on each free module. This is a *space* map, not an arbitrary chain-action. A genuine ordinary unstable based Q-map is used; no replacement by spectra or noncommuting map sums is implicit.

For n>=2, P_n has H_1=B/NB, H_n=ker(d_n) and no intermediate homology because ann(t)=NB, ann(N)=tB. If n odd, H_n=NB. Multiplication N kills H_1 and H_n, so it is a homology ghost. If n even, H_n=tB and N kills that too. For n=1, P_1=B (one term), and N is NONZERO on homology, hence not a ghost. This proves **within this declared spherical family** that the first norm ghost is n=3 (m=1). Do not claim no lower-dimensional ghost from arbitrary finite Q-CW pairs or other maps.

For the stronger derived test, let a degree +1 B-linear nullhomotopy have components h_i = multiplication by u_i ∈B for i=1,...,n−1. Its equations are d_(i+1)u_i+u_(i−1)d_i=N for i=1,...,n, with u_0=u_n=0 and zero outside. At i=1, Nu_1=N forces u_1≡1 mod(t). At i=2, t u_2+Nu_1=N forces t u_2=0, so u_2∈NB. Recursively, odd u_(2j−1)≡1 mod(t), and even u_(2j)∈NB. If n>=3 is odd, its top equation u_(n−1)t=N requires 0=N, contradiction. Thus [N·id_(P_n)]≠0 in D(B). Conversely if n>=2 is even, choosing u_i=1 for odd i and u_i=0 for even i solves EVERY equation, including both ends, so N·id is chain-nullhomotopic. For n=1 it is nonzero already on homology. **Exact algebraic parity criterion:** N is a nonzero derived homology ghost precisely when n is odd and n>=3, for this explicitly declared truncated alternating resolution; even lengths give derived-zero ghosts. Since P_n is bounded free, derived-null iff ordinary B-linear chain-null; no unexamined higher obstruction. Geometric spherical models have odd n only.

## T219: a concrete NO-RECONSTRUCTION theorem

Take q=p=5 and m=1, with weighted C5-actions on S³ whose quotient lens spaces are L(5,1) and L(5,2). Standard linking-pairing classification says these are NOT homotopy equivalent: an unoriented homotopy equivalence would require 2 ≡ ±u² (mod 5) for some u∈(Z/5)^×; squares are {1,4}, so ±squares={1,4}, excluding 2. Ranicki, *Algebraic and Geometric Surgery*, Example 8.45 explicitly gives the pair; this classification is SOURCE-STATED, not claimed a new proof from cellular chains.

Nevertheless the mod-5 cellular free-C5 complexes of the suspended relative pairs are B --(g^v−1)-->B --N--> B (with the two successive maps read in descending degree). For v=1 or 2 or its inverse, (g^v−1)=(g−1)(1+g+...+g^(v−1)) and the second factor has augmentation v≠0 mod5, so is a unit in local B=F5[t]/t^5. By a change of B-basis, BOTH complexes are isomorphic to B --t-->B --t^4-->B. The scalar norm N=t^4 commutes with every change of basis. Thus the (P_3,N) norm-ghost endomorphism pairs, and the corresponding whole-real TWO-active-block derived persistence diagrams, are indistinguishable; the full metric signatures in T218 agree for each a,b,c.

**Hence no invariant that factors only through this perfect F5[C5]-cellular complex equipped with its norm endomorphism (or the resulting derived step diagram) can reconstruct the homotopy type of the lens-space quotient.** This falsifies any S093 'cover reconstruction/classification' claim at precisely that level. It does NOT say no stronger integral, multiplicative, linking-form, k-invariant, or truly unstable equivariant invariant can distinguish the spaces; those ARE potential ways forward. In particular the construction supplies a group-family **detection of some derived ghosts**, not a classification of all covers or all ghosts.
